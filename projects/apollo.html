MITES Summer Intensive — MIT

Apollo 11 3D Animation

A fully original 3D animated recreation of the Apollo 11 Saturn V launch and lunar landing, built from scratch in Autodesk Maya over 6 weeks as part of the MITES summer program at MIT.

Summer 2023 3D Animation & Visual Storytelling
Autodesk Maya 3D Modeling Animation MITES @ MIT Rendering

Program Context

MITES (Minority Introduction to Engineering and Science) is a rigorous six-week residential STEM enrichment program hosted at MIT. Accepted students take college-level courses, work on intensive projects, and engage with researchers and engineers across disciplines. I was selected to participate in the summer of 2023.

As part of the program, I enrolled in a 3D animation and visual storytelling course where the capstone deliverable was a fully original animated short — modeled, rigged, lit, and rendered entirely from scratch with no pre-built assets.

Project Scope & Timeline

  • Duration: 6 weeks, beginning with zero prior Maya experience
  • Software: Autodesk Maya (modeling, rigging, animation, rendering)
  • Subject: Apollo 11 mission — Saturn V launch through lunar landing
  • Deliverable: Fully rendered animated short film, original soundtrack
  • Assets: All 3D models, environments, and materials built from scratch

Animation

The animation covers the full Apollo 11 mission arc: pre-launch on the launchpad, Saturn V ignition and liftoff, stage separation in orbit, lunar transit, and the Eagle's descent to the lunar surface. Each sequence was keyframe-animated and rendered with attention to scale, lighting, and historical accuracy.

Reference Material

Historical footage of the Apollo 11 mission served as the primary visual reference throughout the project, guiding the recreation of the Saturn V rocket geometry, launch sequence timing, and the lunar surface environment.

Technical Challenges & Solutions

  • Zero Prior Experience: Learned Maya fundamentals — polygon modeling, UV unwrapping, material nodes, keyframe animation, and camera rigging — within the first two weeks while simultaneously developing the concept.
  • Scale & Proportion: Accurately representing the Saturn V (363 ft tall) relative to the launchpad and lunar module required careful reference study and scene unit management.
  • Rocket Engine Exhaust: Simulated engine plume effects using Maya's particle and fluid systems — the most technically complex element of the project.
  • Lunar Environment: Built a procedural lunar surface using displacement maps and custom shaders to achieve the correct low-albedo, cratered appearance without texture libraries.
  • Render Optimization: Managed render times on shared lab hardware by optimizing polygon counts, using instancing for repeated geometry, and baking lighting where possible.

Outcome & Reflection

  • Completed a polished, fully rendered animated short within the 6-week program timeline
  • Gained proficiency in Maya from zero — a skill that now informs how I think about 3D geometry in mechanical CAD
  • Presented the final animation to MITES faculty, staff, and peers at the end-of-program showcase
  • Experience deepened appreciation for the intersection of engineering accuracy and visual communication
