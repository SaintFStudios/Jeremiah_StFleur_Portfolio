Course Project (EML2322L)

Compressed Air Engine

Design · Manufacture · Assemble · Test

A complete engineering design cycle project — machined air engine powering a four-bar slicer mechanism, built to tight tolerances on lathe and mill.

Jan 2025 – May 2025 Mechanical Design & Academic
DFM CNC / Lathe Tolerance Analysis Mechanism Design

This academic project walked the full design-manufacture-assemble-test cycle. The compressed air engine serves as a power source driving a rocker four-bar slicing mechanism. Components were machined from aluminum 6061 and UHMW on a manual lathe and mill, held to thou-level fits throughout, then assembled and tested against RPM and budget targets.

Engineering Documentation

DFM drawings, ECNs, MIRs, and material selections produced through the full design cycle

Manufacturing
  • Processes: Lathe turning, milling, drilling, tapping, CNC contouring
  • Engine builds: 3 interchangeable assemblies produced
  • Assembly: Press-fit blade holder; clearance fits for moving parts; sealed air connections
Materials
  • Engine body: Aluminum 6061
  • Slider: UHMW polyethylene
  • Chassis: PETG (3D printed)
  • Hardware: OTS stainless brackets — reduced machining time 30%
DFM & Change Control
  • DFM: Revised drawings with tolerance stack-up analysis
  • ECNs: 5 major design revisions documented
  • MIRs: Quality control records for all machined parts
Performance Results
  • Achieved: 650 RPM at 40 PSI (within target range)
  • Slicer: Successfully cut through test materials at speed
  • Inspection: All parts passed dimensional check within spec
  • Cost: $68 total — $7 under budget
